# EZ-B003 — Hidden Shell Rediscovery Challenge

Protocol version: `1.0.0`. No legacy alias.

EZ-B002 hides a rectangle of the known chart and asks whether the models can
reconstruct the masses inside it. EZ-B003 hides the neighborhood of a *known
shell closure* and asks a harder question:

    after the closure region is withheld, does the reconstructed mass surface
    still show the shell-gap structure that is actually there?

It is the closest early analogue to "can the system discover a stability
structure without being told where it is". It is a **controlled rediscovery
benchmark**, not a discovery claim.

```text
b003-select-challenges -> availability rule + support rule -> preregistered challenges.json
b003-prepare           -> identity-only targets inside one closure neighborhood
b003-freeze            -> KnowledgeFreeze over the eligible nuclei outside it
b003-predict           -> fit + certificates + H0/H1 pair, one sealed run per model
b003-finalize          -> LEDGER_FINALIZED
b003-score             -> unlock closure truth, derive the indicators, rank the peak
```

Experiment-level wrappers: `b003-seal-experiment` then `b003-score-experiment`.
The seal phase writes `CRITERION.json`; the score phase may only read it.

## The boundary — read this before quoting a result

A met criterion is evidence for **one narrow capability**: rediscovery of known
shell-related mass structure under controlled masking.

It is **not**:

- proof that a new magic number exists,
- evidence that a predicted `Z = 154` shell gap exists,
- evidence for an island of stability anywhere.

That later claim requires independent physics-model ensembles, deformation
calculations, fission calculations, decay competition, and far larger
extrapolation uncertainty. Successful mass interpolation described as proof of a
new island of stability is a **stop condition** of this benchmark, not a result.

The statement is not only prose: it travels inside every artifact as
`boundary_rule`, and every criterion verdict carries `scope_rule` next to it.

## Two profiles that never mix

| profile | features | may be quoted as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `discovery` | `Z`, `N`, `A` and preregistered primitive parity terms | a rediscovery result |
| `accuracy` | may later add physics-informed shell features | an accuracy result only |

Pooling the two into one comparison is a protocol error
(`assert_profile_not_mixed`). An accuracy-profile number is never a rediscovery
number: a model told where the shells are has not rediscovered them.

### Feature firewall

`ez-b003-discovery-feature-firewall-v1` is a code-level denylist over normalized
feature names — `magic`, `shell_distance`, `distance_to_20/28/50/82/126`,
`known_closure`, `shell_label`, and their case, hyphen, and camelCase variants.
Normalization is why `Distance-To-82` and `distanceTo82` are both refused.

The denylist is **defense in depth**. The primary protection is the explicit
feature-policy manifest, whose hash enters the split digest, and the firewall is
re-run against the *fitted* model's declared features so a model cannot smuggle a
feature in after the policy was checked.

## Mask geometry

Half-width one, on the closure axis only (`schemas/shell_mask.schema.json`):

```text
neutron closure N0:   N in {N0-1, N0, N0+1},  z_min <= Z <= z_max
proton  closure Z0:   Z in {Z0-1, Z0, Z0+1},  n_min <= N <= n_max
```

Half-width one is the smallest mask that keeps the indicator at the closure a
prediction rather than a lookup, because

```text
delta2n(Z,N0) = 2*B(Z,N0) - B(Z,N0-2) - B(Z,N0+2)
```

withholds `B(Z,N0)` while both two-step neighbors stay in training. Hiding a
single point would leave `N0-1` and `N0+1` in training, and interpolating two
immediate neighbors is not reconstructing shell structure.

The masked span on the *other* axis is the contiguous hull of every chain that
holds an eligible nucleus in the neighborhood — not the hull of the scorable
chains. A shell gap at `N0` is a property of the closure coordinate and is the
same along every chain, so one unmasked chain still carrying its own `N0`
neighborhood would reveal the answer for all the others. Support decides which
chains are *scored*; the neighborhood is hidden wherever it exists.

## Support rule and NOT_EVALUABLE

`ez-b003-support-rule-v1`, preregistered and computed from identities alone.

```text
chain is supported when
    it holds at least one eligible masked target
    the indicator at the closure itself is computable
    the two-step lower neighbor (closure - 2) is eligible and outside the mask
    the two-step upper neighbor (closure + 2) is eligible and outside the mask
    MIN_CHAIN_LENGTH   eligible nuclei outside the mask in the search window
    MIN_PEAK_CANDIDATES window positions have a computable indicator

closure is EVALUABLE when
    MIN_EVALUABLE_CHAINS chains are supported
    and the mask holds at least MIN_TARGETS eligible targets
```

Frozen values: `half_width = 1`, `MIN_CHAIN_LENGTH = 5`, `MIN_TARGETS = 6`,
`MIN_EVALUABLE_CHAINS = 3`, `MIN_PEAK_CANDIDATES = 3`, `PEAK_WINDOW = 6`.

A closure that fails the rule is reported `NOT_EVALUABLE` **with its reasons**,
in `challenges.json`, in the seal, and in the aggregate. It is never silently
omitted, because a benchmark that quietly drops the closures it cannot handle
reports only the closures it can.

Availability set (`schemas/shell_challenge.schema.json`): neutron
`20, 28, 50, 82, 126` and proton `20, 28, 50, 82`. Which members are *produced*
is decided by the support rule alone, never by how well a model does on them.

## Derived observables

```text
B(Z,N)       from predicted mass excess, via binding_energy_MeV
S2n(Z,N)     = B(Z,N) - B(Z,N-2)
S2p(Z,N)     = B(Z,N) - B(Z-2,N)
delta2n(Z,N) = S2n(Z,N) - S2n(Z,N+2) = 2*B(Z,N) - B(Z,N-2) - B(Z,N+2)
delta2p(Z,N) = S2p(Z,N) - S2p(Z+2,N) = 2*B(Z,N) - B(Z-2,N) - B(Z+2,N)
```

No model trains on a derived target binding energy.

These are **derived observables**: they re-express the masses they were computed
from and are not independent evidence from them. Every value carries
`derived = true`, `independent_evidence = false`, its exact input identities, and
the origin of each input (`truth`, `training_truth`, or `prediction`). In Atlas a
truth-derived value is `E2`/`domain`; a value with a prediction among its inputs
is `E3`/`analyst`, because it is a statement about a model rather than about the
chart.

The predicted indicator is a hybrid, fixed by the mask geometry before any hidden
truth is read (`ez-b003-hybrid-derivation-v1`): the sealed prediction at every
masked input, the frozen training mass at every unmasked one.

A large positive local `delta` can indicate shell structure. **Not every local
maximum is a magic number.**

## Discovery metrics

Mass metrics stay required — a rediscovery claim resting on a badly reconstructed
surface is not a rediscovery claim. Per hidden closure and chain:

```text
true_<indicator>            predicted_<indicator>
absolute_<indicator>_error  sign_recovered
local_peak_rank             rank_1 / top_3 / outside_top_3
```

Ranking (`ez-b003-peak-rank-v1`): candidates in the preregistered window are
ordered by predicted indicator value, descending, ties by coordinate ascending.
Candidates share the **parity** of the closure, because the two-nucleon
difference only cancels the pairing term within one parity class, so a
mixed-parity ranking would compare a pairing artifact with a shell gap.

No p-value is reported for peak localization: no null model is preregistered in
v1.

## Preregistered rediscovery criterion

Unlike EZ-B002 characterization, EZ-B003 has a scientific criterion, frozen
before any real closure truth is read. `ez-b003-rediscovery-criterion-v1`:

```text
sign_recovered_fraction        >= 0.75
top_3_fraction                 >= 0.75
rank_1_fraction                >= 0.50
abs(coverage_90 - 0.90)        <= 0.15
```

All four, over supported chains, plus a per-closure report. The calibration term
is there so a model cannot satisfy the shell criterion with uncertainties that
are not honest.

The thresholds were calibrated on the **synthetic mechanics only** and are frozen
in `CRITERION.json` by the *seal* phase, hashed, before any closure truth is
read. The score phase re-derives the digest and refuses to run if it moved.
Changing a threshold requires a new B003 protocol version and a complete rerun
with the old result preserved. A threshold selected after real scoring is a stop
condition, not a result.

## Atlas hypotheses

Two competing structures per masked closure, plus the masking itself as a
`BOUNDARY_CHANGE` intervention:

```text
H0 = no local shell discontinuity at the closure
H1 = a local shell discontinuity at the closure
```

`ez-b003-hypothesis-decision-v1`: a chain supports H1 when the indicator at the
withheld closure is positive and ranks first in the window, and H0 when the
indicator is not positive. A hypothesis becomes `SELECTED_REPRESENTATIVE` for a
surface only on a strict majority of the closure's evaluable chains. Two
resolutions are recorded per closure: one from snapshot truth, which restates
what the known chart contains, and one from the sealed reconstruction, which is
the benchmark question.

The pair is created at *seal* time, before any truth is read, and rebuilt at
score time from the same inputs; a mismatch in the content-addressed identities
is a protocol error. This is evidence bookkeeping. Unrelated Atlas conjectures
are not imported, and nothing here promotes a local maximum to a magic number.

## Order of operations

```text
select challenges -> commit challenges.json
                  -> seal every evaluable closure + freeze CRITERION.json
                  -> commit the seal
                  -> score
```

`SEALED_PREDICTIONS.json` is written with state
`PREDICTIONS_SEALED_CLOSURE_TRUTH_UNREAD` and hashed before any truth inside a
neighborhood is read. Scoring refuses a run whose finalization marker changed
after the seal.

## Stop conditions

STOP if a known closure label reaches a discovery-model feature, if target truth
is used to tune hyperparameters, if a threshold is selected after real scoring,
if successful mass interpolation is described as proof of a new island of
stability, if masks are changed after seeing performance, if a `NOT_EVALUABLE`
closure is silently omitted, or if a sealed run is refit.

## Synthetic benchmark first

WO-10 section 8 requires the mechanics to be validated where the answer is known
by construction, *before* any known closure is scored.
`tests/fixtures/b003/synthetic_shell_chart_v1.mas20` is the EZ-B002 toy surface
plus two injected binding-energy kinks, `-g * max(0, x - x0)`, at `N0 = 50` with
`g_n = 1.5 MeV` and `Z0 = 28` with `g_p = 1.2 MeV`. A kink of that shape puts a
spike of exactly `+2g` into the indicator at the closure and zero everywhere else
in the same parity class, so "did the benchmark find the injected feature" has an
arithmetic answer.

`tests/integration/test_synthetic_b003.py` checks all five mechanics against a
control chart with the kinks moved off the lattice: with the feature present the
withheld closure ranks first in every scored chain, and on the control chart it
does not.

## Committed experiment

`experiments/EZ-B003-v1/` runs the protocol on that synthetic chart, because the
evaluated AME tables are licensed upstream files that stay gitignored. Its
numbers are software evidence for the protocol, not scientific evidence about
nuclei, and its scope is recorded as `synthetic` in every verdict.

**No closure of an evaluated mass table has been scored.** The thresholds are
frozen and `evaluated_mass_table_verdict` is `NOT_YET_SCORED`. Scoring a real
closure is a separate, later act under a new experiment id, and it may not change
a threshold. See `experiments/EZ-B003-v1/PREREGISTRATION.md`.

### The v1 result: every baseline fails

Two closures survive the support rule on the synthetic chart, `neutron-N50` and
`proton-Z28`, and seven are reported `NOT_EVALUABLE` with their reasons. Against
the criterion frozen one commit earlier:

| model | sign | rank-1 | top-3 | cal. error | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `EZ-SEMF-LS-v1` | 0.400 | 0.200 | 0.286 | 0.225 | `CRITERION_NOT_MET` |
| `EZ-GP-DIRECT-v1` | 0.914 | 0.400 | 0.514 | 0.100 | `CRITERION_NOT_MET` |
| `EZ-SEMF-GP-RESIDUAL-v1` | 1.000 | 0.086 | 0.800 | 0.100 | `CRITERION_NOT_MET` |
| required | `>= 0.75` | `>= 0.50` | `>= 0.75` | `<= 0.15` | |

This is a result about the models, not a protocol failure. The mechanics are
verified separately: feed truth back through the sealed pipeline and the withheld
closure ranks first in every scored chain.

The failure is the physically expected one, and the metrics locate it. The
residual model recovers the *sign* of the gap in 35 of 35 chains and puts the
closure in the top 3 in 80% of them, then ranks it first in 8.6%: it detects that
something is there and cannot say precisely where. That is what a smooth
interpolator does to a discontinuity. Training data below and above `N0` differ
in slope by `g`, but *which* of `N0-1, N0, N0+1` the break happened at survives
only in the level offset, recoverable only by a model whose inductive bias admits
a kink. A squared-exponential GP has no such bias and smears a `+3.0 MeV` spike
across the window; mean absolute indicator error runs 1.65 to 7.09 MeV against
features of 2.4 and 3.0 MeV.

`EZ-SEMF-LS-v1` is the clearest reading. Plain SEMF has no shell term, so it
cannot express a shell gap, and it resolves to H0 — no discontinuity at `N = 50` —
while snapshot truth resolves H1. It also fails calibration at 0.225, having
reported 53% coverage from a nominal 90% interval, which is the clause that stops
a confident wrong answer from passing.

Tightening a threshold now, or adopting a kernel chosen because it passes, is a
stop condition rather than a result. The honest reading is that these baselines
cannot rediscover a hidden shell gap from `Z`, `N`, `A` alone — a real negative
result about the models, and still nothing whatsoever about `Z = 154`.
